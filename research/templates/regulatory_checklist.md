# Regulatory verification checklist

Use for tasks of type `verify_licence`. A regulation is **verified** only when a named person has checked it against
the issuing authority's own publication on a recorded date. Anything else is a lead.

## For each licence, registration or approval

1. **Find the authority's own source** — the Act/Rules, the authority's portal, or its fee notification. Not a
   consultant's blog, not an aggregator.
2. Record in *Regulatory*:
   - exact name, type (registration / licence / approval / certification / inspection / ongoing compliance)
   - issuing authority and jurisdiction (central / state / local body) and the location it applies to
   - legal basis (Act, rule or notification number)
   - who must hold it, and **the condition that triggers it** (turnover, capacity, head-count, product type, location)
   - application portal, fee basis, validity and renewal period, typical processing time **as stated by the authority**
   - the source document or URL, and the date you checked it
3. **Map it to the business** with the right applicability:
   - *mandatory* — every unit of this operating model needs it
   - *conditional* — write the condition in full ("food business with annual turnover above the registration limit")
   - *optional* — voluntary certification that helps selling
   - *to be determined* — the default for leads; never shown as a requirement
4. Set a revalidation date. Fees, thresholds and portals change; default is six months.
5. Where the central rule delegates to the state or municipality (trade licence, shops and establishments, fire,
   pollution consent), verify **separately for Pune and for Nanded** — do not assume they match.

## Subsidy and scheme claims

Source documents often assume a capital subsidy. PlanB does **not** model a subsidy until all of these are verified:
the scheme is currently open; the business and the applicant category are eligible; the ceiling and rate; whether it is
back-ended (credit-linked) and when it is actually received. Record the scheme, map eligibility, then record the
subsidy as a model input with its source.

## Do not

- Do not state a fee or a threshold from memory.
- Do not mark verified because two websites agree — they may share a wrong source.
- Do not give legal advice in notes; record what the authority states and where.
